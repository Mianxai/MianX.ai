# Mianx.ai AI Workforce Governance

> **The Mianx.ai AI Workforce Governance Framework defines the authority,
> accountability, decision rights, approvals, delegations, policies, controls,
> reviews, exceptions, evidence, audits, escalation paths, emergency powers,
> and lifecycle gates required to ensure that every AI role, Agent, Team,
> Department, capability, Tool, Model, workflow, memory operation, and
> Production action remains Human-governed, secure, accountable, measurable,
> and aligned with Mianx.ai Company direction.**

# 3. Governance Objective

The primary Governance objective is to ensure that:

```text
Every Material AI Workforce Action
        ↓
Has an Identified Actor
        ↓
Has an Accountable Human Owner
        ↓
Has Valid Authority
        ↓
Has Approved Scope
        ↓
Has Proportionate Risk Controls
        ↓
Produces Verifiable Evidence
        ↓
Can Be Reviewed, Suspended, Rejected, or Reversed
        ↓
Remains Subordinate to Human Governance
```

Governance must make it possible to answer:

- Who authorized the work?
- Who owns the outcome?
- Which Agent performed the work?
- Which Product, Project, Tenant, and environment were involved?
- Which permissions were used?
- Which tools and models were used?
- Which Human approvals were required?
- Which evidence proves the result?
- Who accepted or rejected the outcome?
- How can the action be suspended or reversed?
- Which authority resolves a conflict?

# 5. Governance Authority Hierarchy

The Governance authority hierarchy is:

```text
Applicable Law and Binding Contracts
                ↓
Founder Decisions and Reserved Authority
                ↓
AI Constitution
                ↓
Enterprise Principles
                ↓
Approved Company Vision and Mission
                ↓
Approved Enterprise AI Governance
                ↓
Approved AI Workforce Governance
                ↓
Approved Product and Project Governance
                ↓
Approved Policies, Standards, and Architecture
                ↓
Approved Runtime Configuration
                ↓
Individual Workflow and Task Instructions
```

Lower-level instructions must not override higher authority.

Where authority conflicts:

1. identify the conflicting instructions;
2. identify their authority level;
3. identify the specific applicable scope;
4. suspend high-risk execution where necessary;
5. escalate to the authorized decision-maker;
6. record the resolution;
7. update affected documents and configuration.

# 6. Governance Principles

## 6.1 Human Accountability Is Permanent

AI may perform approved work.

Accountability for material enterprise decisions remains with authorized Humans.

---

## 6.2 AI Cannot Grant Itself Authority

An Agent must not:

- create its own permissions;
- expand its own scope;
- increase its own budget;
- activate unapproved Tools;
- activate unapproved Models;
- approve its own registration;
- approve its own Production activation;
- waive its own controls.

---

## 6.3 Authority Must Be Explicit

Authority must be:

- documented;
- scoped;
- attributable;
- reviewable;
- time-bound where appropriate;
- revocable;
- technically enforceable;
- evidenced.

---

## 6.4 Governance Must Be Proportionate to Risk

Low-risk work may use lightweight controls.

High-risk work requires stronger:

- approval;
- review;
- evidence;
- monitoring;
- Human accountability;
- suspension;
- recovery.

---

## 6.5 No Approval by Silence

Silence, delay, or lack of objection is not approval.

Material approval must be explicit and attributable.

---

## 6.6 No Completion Without Evidence

A Governance gate must not pass based only on an Agent statement.

---

## 6.7 No Runtime Authority From Documentation Alone

A Role Document, policy, plan, or approval proposal does not itself grant
runtime permissions.

---

## 6.8 No Shared Workforce Without Isolation

Shared capability must preserve:

- Product boundaries;
- Project boundaries;
- Tenant boundaries;
- Customer confidentiality;
- environment boundaries;
- cost attribution;
- memory boundaries.

---

## 6.9 Emergency Authority Must Be Controlled

Emergency action may temporarily accelerate decisions.

It must not remove accountability, evidence, expiry, or post-incident review.

---

## 6.10 Exceptions Must Expire

Exceptions must not become permanent silently.

# 29. Risk Classes

| Risk Class | Description | Typical Governance |
|---|---|---|
| `R0` | Informational, read-only, no material action | Owner and logging |
| `R1` | Reversible internal drafting or analysis | Review before reliance |
| `R2` | Controlled internal change | Approval, evidence, tests, rollback |
| `R3` | Customer, Production, Security, privacy, financial, or personal-Data impact | Explicit Human approval and enhanced controls |
| `R4` | Irreversible, constitutional, regulatory, legal, or enterprise-wide impact | Founder or highest designated authority |

Risk must be reassessed when scope changes.

# 30. Approval Classes

## A0 — No Formal Approval Required

Suitable for:

- approved read-only internal lookup;
- low-risk informational tasks;
- non-material analysis.

Logging and ownership may still be required.

## A1 — Owner Review

Suitable for:

- reversible drafting;
- low-risk internal recommendations;
- routine documentation.

## A2 — Domain Approval

Suitable for:

- controlled internal changes;
- department-specific actions;
- approved non-Production workflows.

## A3 — Cross-Functional Approval

Suitable for:

- Security-sensitive changes;
- Data-sensitive changes;
- high-cost changes;
- Customer-facing actions;
- staging or Production preparation.

## A4 — Founder or Highest Authority Approval

Suitable for:

- constitutional change;
- executive Agent activation;
- major financial commitment;
- major legal commitment;
- irreversible action;
- enterprise-wide Production autonomy.

# 118. Governance Maturity Model

## Level 0 — Documented Intent

- Governance concepts documented;
- no runtime enforcement proven.

## Level 1 — Manual Human Governance

- Humans manually review Roles, Agents, Tasks, and approvals.

## Level 2 — System-Assisted Governance

- approval and evidence records are system-managed;
- runtime policy remains limited.

## Level 3 — Enforced Agent Governance

- identity, allocations, permissions, and Agent states are enforced.

## Level 4 — Multi-Team and Multi-Project Governance

- Team, Project, and Tenant controls are enforced.

## Level 5 — Production-Controlled Governance

- monitoring, audit, incidents, suspension, cost, and reviews operate in
  Production.

## Level 6 — Enterprise-Scale Governance

- multiple departments, Products, Customers, and providers remain under
  unified Governance.

## Level 7 — Governed Increasing Autonomy

- higher automation operates with strong Human authority and proven controls.
