# Mianx.ai AI Workforce

> The Mianx.ai AI Workforce is a centrally governed, permission-controlled,
> auditable, measurable, and reusable network of specialized AI roles, agents,
> teams, and departments designed to support approved Mianx.ai Products,
> Projects, Industry Operating Systems, and enterprise operations under Human
> authority.

---

# 1. Document Purpose

This document defines the purpose, scope, structure, authority model, operating
boundaries, lifecycle, and documentation responsibilities of the Mianx.ai AI
Workforce.

It serves as the entry point for all documentation maintained under:

```text
doc/19-ai-workforce/
```

This section explains:

- what the Mianx.ai AI Workforce is;
- why the AI Workforce exists;
- who owns and governs the AI Workforce;
- how AI roles, agents, teams, and departments are structured;
- how documented roles differ from runtime agents;
- how agents receive identity, permissions, tools, models, and authority;
- how agents are allocated to Products, Projects, Tenants, and workflows;
- how Human authority is preserved;
- how agent work is verified;
- how AI Workforce security is maintained;
- how performance and capacity are measured;
- how agents are activated, suspended, improved, and retired;
- how the Workforce supports multiple Projects without mixing protected data;
- how this folder relates to the AI Operating System and Agent Framework;
- which documents are canonical within the AI Workforce domain;
- how the remaining AI Workforce documentation will be completed.

This document defines the AI Workforce documentation and target operating model.

It does not independently prove that any documented agent, team, department,
workflow, tool, model, or runtime capability is implemented, allocated, active,
live-tested, or Production Operational.

# 5. AI Workforce Mission

The mission of the Mianx.ai AI Workforce is to:

- perform approved enterprise work through specialized AI roles;
- multiply Human execution capacity;
- reduce avoidable repetitive work;
- improve delivery consistency;
- increase speed without bypassing Governance;
- support multiple approved Products and Projects;
- preserve Tenant and Project isolation;
- produce verifiable work evidence;
- assist Human decision-makers;
- preserve reusable organizational Knowledge;
- improve workflows through verified learning;
- operate within approved budgets and authority;
- escalate decisions beyond assigned authority;
- remain interruptible and accountable;
- support long-term enterprise creation under Human leadership.

The AI Workforce must not pursue autonomy as an independent objective.

Autonomy may increase only when Governance, security, quality, operations,
monitoring, evidence, recovery, and Human approval are sufficient for the
relevant Risk.

# 34. Risk Classification

AI Workforce work should be classified by Risk.

A proposed model is:

| Risk Class | Description | Typical Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| R0 | Read-only, informational, no material action | Basic identity and logging |
| R1 | Reversible internal drafting or analysis | Review and evidence |
| R2 | Controlled internal change | Approval, tests, rollback |
| R3 | Production, Customer, Security, financial, or personal-data action | Explicit Human approval and enhanced controls |
| R4 | Irreversible, legal, regulatory, constitutional, or enterprise-wide action | Founder or designated highest authority |

Risk classification must consider:

- data sensitivity;
- Customer impact;
- financial exposure;
- legal impact;
- security impact;
- operational impact;
- reversibility;
- scale;
- autonomy;
- external communication;
- Production access.

- permissions;
- allowed roles;
- allowed Products;
- allowed Projects;
- allowed Tenants;
- Risk;
- logging;

A Model is an approved AI inference capability.

Model approval should define:

- provider;
- model identifier;
- approved uses;
- prohibited uses;
- data restrictions;
- quality requirements;
- cost controls;
- fallback behavior;
- monitoring;
- versioning.

## 35.4 Capability

A Capability combines approved skills, tools, models, workflows, authority, and
evidence requirements to perform a defined class of work.

# 43. Escalation Model

Agents must escalate when:

- authority is insufficient;
- instructions conflict;
- higher-authority documents conflict;
- required data is unavailable;
- Risk exceeds the approved threshold;
- Customer impact is material;
- legal interpretation is required;
- privacy may be affected;
- security may be affected;
- a financial commitment is required;
- Production action is high-risk;
- acceptance criteria cannot be met;
- evidence is incomplete;
- destructive action may occur;
- rollback is unavailable;
- Agent confidence is insufficient for the Risk.

A standard escalation path is:

```text
Specialist Agent
      ↓
Manager or Team Lead
      ↓
Department Director
      ↓
C-Suite Owner
      ↓
AI CEO
      ↓
Founder
```

specialist escalation paths.
