# Mianx.ai Enterprise Human and AI Workforce Approval Flow Standard

> **This document defines the governed target-state Approval Flow model for
> Mianx.ai. It establishes Approval identity, Approval Requests, approver
> identity, approver Role, authority, scope, policies, levels, sequential,
> parallel, quorum, unanimous, and conditional approvals, Human approval,
> AI recommendation boundaries, Agent-generated Approval Requests,
> delegation, substitution, separation of duties, conflicts, comments,
> evidence, expiration, timeout, escalation, rejection, withdrawal,
> cancellation, invalidation, supersession, revocation, Workflow gates,
> Task relationships, Customer and Tenant isolation, Security, Privacy,
> Ethics, Compliance, Risk, financial controls, auditability, and
> Production Approval Flow gates.**

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this standard is to ensure that material actions requiring
approval cannot proceed merely because:

- a Workflow reached a step;
- an Agent recommended an action;
- a Team requested an action;
- a requester has sufficient technical access;
- a deadline is urgent;
- an earlier similar action was approved;
- an approver failed to respond;
- a system assumes consent.

Approval Flow exists to answer:

```text
WHO HAS AUTHORITY
TO APPROVE WHAT
FOR WHICH SCOPE
UNDER WHICH CONDITIONS
FOR HOW LONG
WITH WHAT EVIDENCE?
```

This standard governs:

- Approval Requests;
- Human approvals;
- approval policies;
- approval authority;
- approval scope;
- approval levels;
- multi-stage approval;
- Workflow gates;
- Task approvals;
- Product approvals;
- Project approvals;
- Customer approvals;
- Tenant approvals;
- Security approvals;
- Privacy approvals;
- Ethics approvals;
- Compliance approvals;
- Risk approvals;
- financial approvals where governed;
- approval evidence;
- approval lifecycle.

This document does not independently:

- create Founder authority;
- create Human authority;
- grant an Agent approval power;
- execute an approved action;
- verify execution;
- approve Production operation;
- activate a Workflow;
- appoint a Role holder;
- authorize Customer or Tenant access.

# 4. Foundational Approval Principle

```text
Valid Approval Request
+
Exact Subject
+
Exact Scope
+
Valid Approver Identity
+
Valid Approver Role
+
Valid Approval Authority
+
Satisfied Policy Requirements
+
No Prohibited Conflict
+
Required Evidence
=
Eligible Approval Decision
```

An eligible Approval Decision is not automatically an approved result.

---

# 5. Core Approval Rule

An action requiring approval must remain blocked until the exact required
approval state exists.

```text
REQUIRED_APPROVAL_NOT_PRESENT
=
ACTION_BLOCKED
```

Silence, delay, Agent confidence, urgency, or system convenience must not be
treated as implicit approval.

# 8. Approval Authority

Approval authority defines:

- who may approve;
- what they may approve;
- which scope applies;
- maximum Risk level;
- maximum financial value where applicable;
- Customer scope;
- Tenant scope;
- duration;
- delegation rights;
- restrictions.

Approval authority must be explicit and verifiable.

---

# 9. Founder Sovereignty

Founder-reserved approvals may include:

- Company identity changes;
- constitutional Governance changes;
- executive authority changes;
- material strategic commitments;
- existential Risk decisions;
- material autonomous-system authority changes;
- other matters explicitly reserved to Founder authority.

No Workflow, Human, or AI Agent may fabricate Founder approval.

---

# 10. Qualified Human Accountability

Qualified Human approval may be mandatory for:

- Production changes;
- privileged Security actions;
- sensitive Privacy actions;
- material legal commitments;
- material Compliance exceptions;
- financial commitments;
- high-impact Customer actions;
- high-impact Tenant actions;
- high-autonomy AI operations;
- irreversible external actions.

---

# 11. Approval Flow Engine Boundary

The Approval Flow Engine may:

- create or register Approval Requests;
- resolve Approval Policies;
- identify required approvers;
- validate authority;
- collect approval outcomes;
- enforce quorum;
- enforce expiry;
- block or release Workflow gates;
- preserve evidence.

It may not:

- invent approver authority;
- silently convert recommendations to approvals;
- grant Founder authority;
- self-approve prohibited actions;
- execute the approved action merely because approval exists.
