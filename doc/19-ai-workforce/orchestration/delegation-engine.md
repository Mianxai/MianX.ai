# Mianx.ai AI Workforce Delegation Engine

> **This document defines the governed target-state framework through which
> authorized Humans, AI Agents, Teams, Departments, Products, Projects, and
> orchestration services may transfer bounded execution responsibility without
> transferring Founder sovereignty, final accountability, unrestricted
> authority, legal responsibility, financial authority, Security obligations,
> privacy obligations, or Production control.**

# 3. Strategic Alignment

The Delegation Engine operates inside the exact Mianx.ai hierarchy:

```text
Mianx.ai Company and Governance
↓
MianX Core Platform
↓
Mianx.ai AI Operating System
↓
Shared AI Workforce
↓
Industry Operating Systems
↓
Customer Editions
↓
Autonomous Enterprise Creation at Scale
```

Delegation must preserve this hierarchy.

It must not allow:

- a Customer Edition to delegate Company Governance authority;
- an Industry Operating System to delegate Core Platform ownership;
- a Project Agent to delegate enterprise-level authority;
- an Agent to delegate Founder-reserved decisions;
- a Team to delegate work outside its approved Product or Project;
- one Tenant to delegate work into another Tenant;
- a delegate to expand its own scope;
- a delegator to grant authority it does not possess.

# 7. Delegation Definition

Delegation is the governed transfer of bounded responsibility to perform
specified work from an authorized delegator to an eligible delegate.

A valid delegation must define:

- exact work;
- exact scope;
- exact delegate;
- exact authority;
- exact limits;
- exact due condition;
- exact expiry;
- exact acceptance criteria;
- exact review requirements;
- exact evidence requirements.

---

# 8. Delegation Authority

Delegation authority defines who may delegate:

- which work;
- to which delegate types;
- within which organization;
- within which Department;
- within which Product;
- within which Project;
- within which Customer;
- within which Tenant;
- within which environment;
- up to which Risk class;
- with which financial limit;
- with which sub-delegation rights.

Delegation authority must be:

- explicit;
- current;
- scope-bounded;
- time-bounded;
- evidence-backed;
- revocable;
- subordinate to Founder and Governance authority.

---

# 9. Delegator

A delegator is the authorized Human, Agent, Team, Department, workflow, or
orchestration service that creates a delegation request.

A delegator must have:

- valid identity;
- valid Role;
- valid authority;
- valid scope;
- valid lifecycle state;
- valid Product and Project relationship;
- current delegation rights;
- no active suspension affecting the delegation;
- sufficient evidence.

---

# 10. Delegate

A delegate is the Human, Agent, Team, Department, or governed service that
accepts bounded responsibility for delegated work.

A delegate must have:

- valid identity;
- eligible lifecycle state;
- required Role;
- required Skills;
- required capabilities;
- approved Tools and Models;
- approved memory access;
- sufficient capacity;
- valid environment access;
- no disqualifying conflict;
- no active suspension affecting the work.

---

# 11. Accountable Owner

The Accountable Owner remains responsible for ensuring:

- work remains authorized;
- the delegate remains eligible;
- progress is monitored;
- escalation occurs;
- evidence is preserved;
- completion is verified;
- outcomes are accepted;
- failures are addressed.

The Accountable Owner may be different from the delegator.

---

# 12. Delegation Owner

The Delegation Owner administers the delegation record and lifecycle.

Responsibilities may include:

- validation;
- tracking;
- expiry monitoring;
- renewal;
- escalation;
- revocation;
- closure;
- evidence retention.

The Delegation Owner does not automatically become the accountable business
owner.

# 17. Delegation Classes

The target-state delegation classes are:

```text
DC-0 — Informational or Advisory Delegation

DC-1 — Routine Low-Risk Task Delegation

DC-2 — Controlled Functional Delegation

DC-3 — Cross-Team or Cross-Department Delegation

DC-4 — Critical or Production-Affecting Delegation

DC-5 — Founder-Reserved or Non-Delegable Authority
```

---

# 18. DC-0 — Advisory Delegation

DC-0 may include:

- research;
- analysis;
- summarization;
- option generation;
- evidence collection;
- draft preparation;
- non-binding recommendation.

DC-0 must not be interpreted as authority to approve or execute material
actions.

---

# 19. DC-1 — Routine Task Delegation

DC-1 may include:

- low-risk Task execution;
- approved Data transformation;
- routine documentation;
- standard testing;
- approved scheduling;
- approved monitoring;
- low-risk internal communication.

DC-1 still requires valid identity, scope, capacity, and evidence.

---

# 20. DC-2 — Controlled Functional Delegation

DC-2 may include:

- bounded Product work;
- bounded Project work;
- functional analysis;
- approved Tool execution;
- approved workflow operation;
- Team-level review;
- bounded Customer support work.

DC-2 normally requires functional-owner controls.

---

# 21. DC-3 — Cross-Team or Cross-Department Delegation

DC-3 may involve:

- multiple Teams;
- multiple Departments;
- shared capabilities;
- cross-functional ownership;
- material dependencies;
- shared capacity;
- shared cost;
- multiple review authorities.

DC-3 requires explicit primary accountability.

---

# 22. DC-4 — Critical Delegation

DC-4 may include:

- Production-affecting work;
- critical Security response;
- critical privacy response;
- material Customer action;
- material financial action;
- high-impact incident response;
- destructive or irreversible operations.

DC-4 requires qualified Human authority and mandatory control reviews.

---

# 23. DC-5 — Non-Delegable Authority

DC-5 includes authority that must not be delegated through routine workforce
orchestration.

Examples include:

- Founder sovereignty;
- AI Constitution approval;
- Founder identity;
- unrestricted executive appointment;
- unrestricted legal signature authority;
- unrestricted financial authority;
- unrestricted enterprise Production activation;
- unrestricted residual Risk acceptance;
- ownership change;
- company sale;
- company dissolution.
