# Mianx.ai AI Workforce Conflict Resolution Framework

> **This document defines the governed target-state process through which
> conflicts among Humans, AI Agents, Teams, Departments, Products, Projects,
> Customers, Tenants, workflows, Tools, Models, memory systems, priorities,
> evidence, and decision authorities are detected, registered, contained,
> analyzed, negotiated, mediated, arbitrated, escalated, decided, implemented,
> verified, appealed, reopened, closed, and audited while preserving Founder
> sovereignty, Human accountability, Security, privacy, legal obligations,
> financial controls, evidence, and Production safety.**

# 7. Conflict Definition

A conflict exists when two or more valid positions, claims, instructions,
priorities, authorities, resources, requirements, outputs, or obligations are
materially incompatible and the incompatibility:

- blocks authorized work;
- threatens safety;
- threatens quality;
- threatens Customer commitments;
- threatens Product or Project scope;
- threatens Security or privacy;
- creates legal or financial exposure;
- causes duplicated or contradictory execution;
- creates unresolved accountability;
- requires authoritative intervention.

---

# 8. Disagreement Definition

A disagreement is a difference in:

- opinion;
- interpretation;
- preference;
- design;
- approach;
- estimate;
- recommendation;
- priority;
- expected outcome.

A disagreement becomes a formal conflict when it materially blocks or
threatens governed work.

---

# 9. Conflict Incident Boundary

A conflict may create an incident when it causes:

- unauthorized execution;
- Production failure;
- Customer harm;
- Security exposure;
- privacy exposure;
- financial loss;
- legal breach;
- Data corruption;
- cross-Tenant leakage;
- material operational disruption.

The conflict case and incident record must remain separately identifiable.

---

# 10. Conflict Authority

Conflict authority defines who may:

- register a conflict;
- validate a conflict;
- impose temporary controls;
- appoint a facilitator;
- appoint a mediator;
- appoint an arbitrator;
- request evidence;
- pause affected work;
- restrict participants;
- decide a resolution;
- approve implementation;
- verify closure;
- hear an appeal;
- reopen a case.

Conflict authority must be:

- explicit;
- current;
- scope-bounded;
- domain-bounded;
- Risk-bounded;
- time-bounded where delegated;
- evidence-backed;
- revocable.

---

# 11. Founder Authority Boundary

The Founder retains final authority over:

- Founder-reserved matters;
- Company identity;
- enterprise Vision;
- AI Constitution;
- major strategy;
- executive authority conflicts;
- material ownership conflicts;
- enterprise-wide Risk appetite;
- unrestricted Production authority;
- existential enterprise conflicts.

No AI Agent, mediator, arbitrator, executive majority, Department, Team, or
Customer may override Founder-reserved authority.

---

# 12. Human Accountability

Qualified Human accountability is mandatory for conflicts involving:

- material financial exposure;
- legal obligations;
- employment matters;
- Customer contractual commitments;
- critical Security;
- critical privacy;
- regulated activities;
- destructive Production actions;
- material residual Risk;
- Founder-reserved matters.

AI support does not remove Human accountability.

---

# 13. AI Conflict-Support Boundary

AI Agents may support conflict resolution through:

- conflict detection;
- issue classification;
- statement summarization;
- evidence indexing;
- contradiction detection;
- policy comparison;
- timeline reconstruction;
- option generation;
- impact analysis;
- negotiation support;
- mediation support;
- action tracking;
- monitoring;
- escalation preparation.

AI Agents must not independently:

- issue final binding rulings for material conflicts;
- create their own authority;
- fabricate agreement;
- fabricate Founder approval;
- suppress dissent;
- alter evidence;
- select facts based on preferred outcome;
- accept legal liability;
- accept material financial liability;
- accept material residual Risk;
- reactivate suspended work.

# 17. Conflict Classes

The target-state Conflict Classes are:

```text
CF-0 — Informational Inconsistency

CF-1 — Routine Work Disagreement

CF-2 — Controlled Functional Conflict

CF-3 — Cross-Team, Cross-Department, or Cross-Project Conflict

CF-4 — Critical Customer, Financial, Security, Privacy, Legal, or Production Conflict

CF-5 — Founder-Reserved, Existential, or Enterprise Authority Conflict
```

---

# 18. CF-0 — Informational Inconsistency

CF-0 may include:

- inconsistent labels;
- duplicate references;
- minor version ambiguity;
- non-material interpretation differences;
- missing non-critical metadata.

CF-0 should be corrected without unnecessary escalation.

---

# 19. CF-1 — Routine Work Disagreement

CF-1 may include:

- implementation preference;
- routine Task priority;
- low-risk estimate difference;
- documentation interpretation;
- non-material handoff disagreement;
- low-impact quality disagreement.

CF-1 may be resolved through clarification or facilitation.

---

# 20. CF-2 — Controlled Functional Conflict

CF-2 may include:

- Team ownership;
- functional priority;
- capability selection;
- Tool selection;
- Model selection;
- workflow responsibility;
- moderate capacity competition;
- Product backlog conflict.

CF-2 requires a valid functional resolution authority.

---

# 21. CF-3 — Cross-Organizational Conflict

CF-3 may involve:

- multiple Teams;
- multiple Departments;
- multiple Projects;
- multiple Products;
- shared capacity;
- conflicting ownership;
- conflicting Customer commitments;
- shared-service prioritization;
- cross-functional decisions.

CF-3 requires one primary accountable authority and formal evidence.

---

# 22. CF-4 — Critical Conflict

CF-4 may involve:

- Production operation;
- critical Security;
- critical privacy;
- material Customer impact;
- material financial exposure;
- legal obligations;
- regulatory obligations;
- destructive actions;
- business continuity.

CF-4 requires qualified Human authority and immediate containment.

---

# 23. CF-5 — Founder-Reserved Conflict

CF-5 includes:

- Founder authority;
- Company identity;
- AI Constitution;
- ownership;
- company sale;
- company dissolution;
- executive appointment conflict;
- enterprise strategy conflict;
- unrestricted Production autonomy;
- existential enterprise Risk.

CF-5 requires Founder authority.

# 30. Resolution Authority

The Resolution Authority is the valid Human or Governance authority permitted
to approve or issue the final resolution.

- conflict class;
- conflict severity;
- affected scope;
- applicable decision rights;
- Founder-reserved boundaries;
- financial authority;
- legal authority;
- Security authority;
- privacy authority;
- Production authority.

# 31. Authority Precedence

```text
1. Applicable Law and Binding Regulatory Obligation

2. Founder Authority and Approved AI Constitution

3. Approved Enterprise Governance

5. Approved Enterprise Strategy and Decisions

6. Approved Product, Project, Customer, and Tenant Authority

7. Approved Department, Team, Workflow, and Role Authority

8. Approved Operational Procedures and Local Preferences
```

Precedence does not remove the need for evidence.
