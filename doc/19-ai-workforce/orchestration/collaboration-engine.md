# Mianx.ai AI Workforce Collaboration Engine

> **This document defines the governed target-state framework through which
> authorized Humans, AI Agents, Teams, Departments, Products, Projects, and
> orchestration services may work together toward shared outcomes while
> preserving participant identity, Role boundaries, contribution attribution,
> Founder sovereignty, Human accountability, Product and Project ownership,
> Customer and Tenant isolation, Security, privacy, cost control, evidence,
> review, and Production safeguards.**

---

# 3. Strategic Alignment

The Collaboration Engine operates inside the exact Mianx.ai hierarchy:

```text
Mianx.ai Company and Governance
↓
MianX Core Platform
↓
Mianx.ai AI Operating System
↓
Shared AI Workforce
↓
Industry Operating Systems
↓
Customer Editions
↓
Autonomous Enterprise Creation at Scale
```

Collaboration must preserve this hierarchy.

It must not allow:

- a Customer Edition participant to control Company Governance;
- an Industry Operating System Team to redefine the Core Platform;
- a Project participant to expand enterprise authority;
- a collaboration group to override Founder-reserved decisions;
- one Product to access another Product’s restricted information;
- one Project to consume another Project’s private context without authority;
- one Customer or Tenant to access another Customer or Tenant;
- a participant majority to create authority that does not exist;
- an Agent group to activate itself.

# 7. Collaboration Definition

Collaboration is the governed process through which two or more eligible
participants contribute coordinated work toward one approved shared objective.

Valid collaboration requires:

- an approved objective;
- defined participants;
- defined Roles;
- bounded scope;
- controlled channels;
- shared-context rules;
- contribution ownership;
- synchronization rules;
- review;
- conflict handling;
- evidence;
- acceptance.

---

# 8. Collaboration Authority

Collaboration authority defines who may:

- request collaboration;
- approve a collaboration session;
- invite participants;
- remove participants;
- assign collaboration Roles;
- approve shared context;
- approve shared memory;
- approve Tool and Model usage;
- change collaboration scope;
- approve merge rules;
- accept the final outcome;
- suspend or close collaboration.

Collaboration authority must be:

- explicit;
- scope-bounded;
- Product-specific;
- Project-specific;
- Customer-specific where applicable;
- Tenant-specific where applicable;
- environment-specific;
- time-bounded;
- evidence-backed;
- revocable.

---

# 9. Founder and Human Authority Boundary

The Collaboration Engine must not:

- replace Founder sovereignty;
- approve Founder-reserved matters;
- create executive authority;
- create legal authority;
- create unrestricted financial authority;
- accept material residual Risk;
- create unrestricted Production authority;
- remove required Human approval;
- convert Agent consensus into Founder approval.

Human accountability remains required where applicable.

# 13. Collaboration Classes

The target-state collaboration classes are:

```text
CC-0 — Informational Collaboration

CC-1 — Routine Low-Risk Collaboration

CC-2 — Controlled Functional Collaboration

CC-3 — Cross-Team or Cross-Department Collaboration

CC-4 — Critical or Production-Affecting Collaboration

CC-5 — Founder-Reserved or Non-Collaborative Authority
```

---

# 14. CC-0 — Informational Collaboration

CC-0 may include:

- information exchange;
- research sharing;
- clarification;
- observation sharing;
- draft review;
- non-binding advice.

CC-0 does not create approval or execution authority.

---

# 15. CC-1 — Routine Collaboration

CC-1 may include:

- documentation work;
- low-risk analysis;
- standard testing;
- routine design review;
- internal knowledge preparation;
- bounded Team coordination.

CC-1 still requires valid participants and scope.

---

# 16. CC-2 — Controlled Functional Collaboration

CC-2 may include:

- Product feature design;
- Project planning;
- engineering implementation planning;
- quality review;
- approved Customer-support analysis;
- controlled Data analysis;
- capability design.

CC-2 normally requires functional-owner review.

---

# 17. CC-3 — Cross-Team or Cross-Department Collaboration

CC-3 may involve:

- multiple Teams;
- multiple Departments;
- shared capabilities;
- shared capacity;
- Product and Project dependencies;
- multiple review authorities;
- shared costs;
- formal handoffs.

CC-3 requires one primary accountable owner.

---

# 18. CC-4 — Critical Collaboration

CC-4 may involve:

- Production incidents;
- major Customer incidents;
- Security incidents;
- privacy incidents;
- material financial decisions;
- major Product releases;
- high-risk architecture changes;
- critical legal or compliance work.

CC-4 requires qualified Human authority and mandatory control participation.

---

# 19. CC-5 — Non-Collaborative Reserved Authority

Certain decisions may receive collaborative input but cannot be decided through
routine collaboration.

Examples include:

- Founder sovereignty;
- AI Constitution approval;
- ownership change;
- company sale;
- company dissolution;
- unrestricted executive appointment;
- unrestricted legal signature authority;
- enterprise-wide Production activation;
- unrestricted residual Risk acceptance.
