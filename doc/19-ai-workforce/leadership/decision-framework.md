# Mianx.ai Enterprise Decision Framework

> **This document defines the governed target-state framework through which
> Mianx.ai decisions are proposed, researched, analyzed, challenged, reviewed,
> approved, rejected, deferred, executed, verified, monitored, revoked,
> superseded, and archived while preserving Founder sovereignty, Human
> accountability, explicit authority, evidence, Risk controls, and bounded AI
> decision support.**

---

# 3. Strategic Alignment

The Decision Framework governs decisions across the exact Mianx.ai hierarchy:

```text
Mianx.ai Company and Governance
↓
MianX Core Platform
↓
Mianx.ai AI Operating System
↓
Shared AI Workforce
↓
Industry Operating Systems
↓
Customer Editions
↓
Autonomous Enterprise Creation at Scale
```

Every material decision must identify where it belongs within this hierarchy.

A decision affecting a lower layer must not silently redefine a higher layer.

Examples:

- a RestaurantOS Product decision must not redefine Mianx.ai company identity;
- a PoultryOS Project decision must not redefine enterprise strategy;
- a Customer Edition decision must not expand Shared AI Workforce authority;
- an Agent workflow decision must not change Founder-reserved Governance;
- a Tool action must not create executive authority.

# 4. Foundational Decision Principle

The foundational rule is:

```text
Valid Authority
+
Complete Decision Context
+
Required Review
+
Explicit Approval
+
Controlled Execution
+
Independent Verification
+
Auditable Evidence
=
Governed Decision
```

If one mandatory component is absent, the decision must remain:

```text
PENDING

CONDITIONAL

INVALID

RESTRICTED

OR

UNVERIFIED
```

according to the applicable rule.

# 5. Founder Sovereignty

The Founder remains the highest enterprise decision authority within the
Mianx.ai Governance model, subject to applicable law and formally adopted
Governance obligations.

Founder sovereignty must remain:

- explicit;
- attributable;
- evidence-backed;
- non-transferable through silent automation;
- protected from AI self-expansion;
- protected from informal majority override;
- protected from workflow bypass.

---

```text

Major Organizational Restructuring

Ownership Change

Merger or Acquisition

Company Sale

Company Dissolution

Critical Executive Succession

Final Executive Conflict Resolution

Material Founder Authority Delegation
```

A Founder-reserved decision must not be approved by:

- an AI executive Agent;
- an executive majority without Founder authority;
- a Department;
- a Product owner;
- a Project owner;
- a Team Lead;
- an automated workflow;
- a Model output;
- an external provider.

# 7. Human Accountability

A qualified Human owner must remain accountable for decisions involving:

- enterprise strategy;
- material finance;
- legal commitments;
- employment or Human-impacting actions;
- Customer contractual commitments;
- material Security Risk;
- material privacy Risk;
- irreversible Production actions;
- destructive actions;
- regulated obligations;
- material residual Risk acceptance.

AI support does not remove Human accountability.

AI Agents may support decisions through:

- research;
- Data collection;
- analysis;
- comparison;
- scenario modelling;
- option generation;
- recommendation drafting;
- Risk identification;
- contradiction detection;
- policy checking;
- evidence assembly;
- impact analysis;
- action tracking;
- monitoring;
- escalation preparation.

AI Agents must not independently:

- approve their own recommendations;
- create their own authority;
- expand their own permissions;
- fabricate Human approval;
- fabricate Founder approval;
- accept material Risk without authority;
- sign contracts;
- make unrestricted financial commitments;
- activate unrestricted Production operation;
- revoke Founder decisions;
- suppress required escalation;
- remove their own suspension.

# 15. Decision Classes

The target-state Decision Classes are:

```text
D0 — Informational Determination

D1 — Routine Operational Decision

D2 — Controlled Functional Decision

D3 — Material Cross-Functional Decision

D4 — Critical Executive Decision

D5 — Founder-Reserved or Existential Decision
```

---

# 16. D0 — Informational Determination

A D0 determination:

- records an observation;
- classifies information;
- does not authorize a material action;
- does not create a commitment;
- does not change authority;
- does not alter Production state.

Examples:

- classifying a report;
- identifying a missing field;
- recording a non-material status;
- recognizing an expired draft.

---

# 17. D1 — Routine Operational Decision

A D1 decision may include:

- approved Task sequencing;
- low-risk scheduling;
- routine assignment within valid capacity;
- non-material workflow routing;
- standard evidence request;
- approved retry within policy.

D1 authority must still be explicit.

---

# 18. D2 — Controlled Functional Decision

A D2 decision may include:

- Department-level prioritization;
- Team resource allocation;
- controlled Tool selection;
- bounded Model routing;
- low-risk Product backlog prioritization;
- non-Production workflow changes.

D2 decisions require functional ownership and review.

---

# 19. D3 — Material Cross-Functional Decision

A D3 decision may affect:

- multiple Departments;
- multiple Products;
- multiple Projects;
- a material Customer commitment;
- material budget;
- material Data access;
- material workflow authority;
- material capacity allocation.

D3 decisions normally require cross-functional review.

---

# 20. D4 — Critical Executive Decision

A D4 decision may affect:

- enterprise strategy;
- major Product direction;
- major Customer impact;
- major financial exposure;
- critical Security Risk;
- critical privacy Risk;
- legal commitments;
- Production authorization;
- enterprise continuity.

D4 decisions require executive authority and mandatory control reviews.

---

# 21. D5 — Founder-Reserved Decision

A D5 decision includes Founder-reserved or existential matters.

D5 decisions require:

- Founder authority;
- complete decision brief;
- material Risk review;
- required legal or financial review;
- explicit Founder decision record;
- preserved evidence.
