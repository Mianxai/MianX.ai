# 8. Responsibility Definition

Responsibility identifies who is expected to perform, contribute to, review,
approve, verify, accept, monitor, escalate, or evidence a governed subject.

A responsibility assignment must define:

- subject;
- outcome;
- Role;
- assignee;
- responsibility type;
- scope;
- authority;
- constraints;
- effective dates;
- escalation;
- evidence.

---

# 9. Accountability Definition

Accountability identifies the one primary owner who remains answerable for the
successful Governance and outcome of a material subject.

Accountability includes responsibility for:

- objective clarity;
- valid assignments;
- capacity;
- authority;
- quality;
- Risk;
- escalation;
- acceptance readiness;
- evidence;
- closure.

The Accountable Owner may assign work but remains accountable unless a valid
higher-authority reassignment is approved.

---

# 10. Responsibility Authority

Responsibility authority defines who may:

- create an assignment;
- approve an assignment;
- change an assignment;
- delegate execution;
- appoint a temporary assignee;
- appoint an acting owner;
- revoke an assignment;
- suspend an assignment;
- resolve an assignment conflict;
- approve a separation-of-duties exception;
- close an assignment.

Responsibility authority must be:

- explicit;
- current;
- scope-bounded;
- Product-specific;
- Project-specific;
- Customer-specific where applicable;
- Tenant-specific where applicable;
- environment-specific;
- Risk-bounded;
- time-bounded where delegated;
- evidence-backed;
- revocable.

---

# 11. Founder Responsibility Boundary

The Founder retains primary accountability or final authority for:

- Company Vision;
- Company Mission;
- Company identity;
- ownership;
- AI Constitution;
- major enterprise strategy;
- major executive appointments;
- major organizational restructuring;
- enterprise Risk appetite;
- unrestricted Production activation;
- company sale;
- company dissolution;
- existential enterprise decisions.

Founder-reserved accountability must not be transferred to:

- an AI Agent;
- an executive majority;
- a Department;
- a Product;
- a Project;
- a Customer;
- a workflow;
- a provider.

---

# 12. Human Accountability Boundary

Qualified Human accountability is mandatory for:

- material financial decisions;
- legal commitments;
- employment decisions;
- Customer contractual commitments;
- critical Security decisions;
- critical privacy decisions;
- regulated activity;
- destructive Production actions;
- material residual Risk;
- Founder-reserved matters.

An Agent may prepare, execute, review, or monitor bounded work but cannot
silently become the legally or ethically accountable Human.


# 18. Accountable — A

The Accountable Owner:

- owns the governed outcome;
- ensures scope clarity;
- ensures valid performers;
- ensures capacity;
- resolves normal blockers;
- ensures review and approval;
- ensures escalation;
- ensures evidence;
- remains answerable for closure.

A material subject must normally have exactly one `A`.

---

# 19. Responsible — R

A Responsible Performer:

- executes assigned work;
- produces required output;
- follows scope;
- follows controls;
- reports progress;
- reports limitations;
- preserves evidence;
- escalates blockers.

A subject may have multiple Responsible Performers.

---

# 20. Consulted — C

A Consulted Party:

- provides specialist input;
- reviews assumptions;
- advises on consequences;
- contributes before a decision or action;
- may raise objections or Risks.

Consultation does not grant approval authority.

---

# 21. Informed — I

An Informed Party:

- receives approved information;
- is not required to perform or approve;
- may need awareness for downstream work;
- must respect confidentiality.

Information receipt does not create accountability.

---

# 22. Reviewer — RV

A Reviewer evaluates:

- completeness;
- quality;
- compliance;
- correctness;
- scope;
- evidence;
- Risks;
- readiness.

A Reviewer may recommend approval, rejection, correction, or escalation.

---

# 23. Approver — AP

An Approver confirms that a governed action or output may proceed within an
approved scope.

Approval must define:

- approver identity;
- authority;
- scope;
- conditions;
- effective time;
- expiry;
- evidence.

Approval does not necessarily verify technical correctness.

---

# 24. Verifier — V

A Verifier independently confirms whether:

- requirements were met;
- controls operated;
- evidence is valid;
- output matches approved scope;
- implementation behaves as claimed.

A critical output should not be solely verified by its primary performer.

---

# 25. Acceptance Owner — AC

The Acceptance Owner determines whether an outcome satisfies the approved
business, Product, Project, Customer, Tenant, or operational acceptance
criteria.

Acceptance does not automatically authorize Production.

---

# 26. Escalation Owner — E

The Escalation Owner receives unresolved:

- blockers;
- authority gaps;
- capacity gaps;
- conflicts;
- incidents;
- Risk;
- deadline breaches;
- control failures.

The Escalation Owner must have valid scope and authority.

---

# 27. Evidence Owner — EO

The Evidence Owner ensures:

- evidence identity;
- completeness;
- integrity;
- classification;
- access;
- retention;
- traceability;
- linkage to the governed subject.

Evidence ownership does not imply business accountability.

---

# 28. Decision Owner — DO

The Decision Owner is authorized to make a defined decision.

The Decision Owner must remain separate from:

- report recipient;
- recommendation author;
- ordinary contributor;
- observer.

A recommendation does not become a decision without the Decision Owner.

---

# 29. Control Owner — CO

The Control Owner is accountable for:

- control definition;
- control implementation;
- control operation;
- monitoring;
- evidence;
- exception handling;
- control improvement.

The process owner and control owner may require separation.

---

# 30. Data Owner — DA

The Data Owner is accountable for:

- Data purpose;
- Data classification;
- access;
- quality expectations;
- retention;
- sharing;
- Customer scope;
- Tenant scope;
- legal and privacy obligations.

The Data Steward may operate controls but does not automatically own the Data.

---

# 31. Steward — ST

A Steward maintains a governed subject on behalf of its owner.

Stewardship may include:

- metadata;
- quality;
- records;
- processes;
- coordination;
- evidence;
- lifecycle administration.

Stewardship does not replace accountability.

---

# 32. Observer — OBS

An Observer may:

- view approved information;
- monitor proceedings;
- support transparency;
- support audit.

An Observer must not silently become a participant, approver, or decision
owner.

# 34. One-Accountable-Owner Rule

For every material:

- Product;
- Project;
- Customer Edition;
- Tenant;
- Department;
- Team;
- workflow;
- decision;
- Risk;
- incident;
- release;
- Production environment;
- control;
- accepted outcome;

there must be one primary Accountable Owner.

Where law or contract requires joint accountability, the assignment must
identify:

- joint authority basis;
- exact shared scope;
- dispute mechanism;
- final escalation authority;
- evidence.

# 35. Responsibility Coverage Rule

Every material subject must identify at minimum:

```text
1 Accountable Owner

1 or More Responsible Performers

0 or More Consulted Parties

0 or More Informed Parties

At Least 1 Escalation Owner

Evidence Ownership

Review, Approval, Verification, and Acceptance Roles Where Required
```

# 36. Founder Responsibilities

The Founder may be:

# 74. Separation of Duties

Separation of Duties prevents one party from controlling incompatible stages
of a material process.

Common separated stages include:

```text
REQUEST

REVIEW

APPROVAL

EXECUTION

VERIFICATION

ACCEPTANCE

PAYMENT

RECONCILIATION

AUDIT

RISK ACCEPTANCE

PRODUCTION AUTHORIZATION
```

---

# 75. Separation-of-Duties Principle

```text
No Single Participant Should Unilaterally Request, Approve, Execute, Verify,
Accept, and Audit the Same High-Risk Action.
```

The required degree of separation depends on:

- Risk;
- financial value;
- Customer impact;
- Tenant impact;
- Security impact;
- privacy impact;
- legal impact;
- reversibility;
- Production impact.

---

# 76. Prohibited Responsibility Combinations

The following combinations should normally be prohibited for material work:

```text
REQUESTOR + SOLE APPROVER

PRIMARY PERFORMER + SOLE VERIFIER

PAYMENT REQUESTOR + PAYMENT APPROVER + RECONCILER

ACCESS REQUESTOR + ACCESS APPROVER

SECURITY CONTROL IMPLEMENTER + SOLE CONTROL VERIFIER

INCIDENT CAUSE OWNER + SOLE INCIDENT CLOSURE VERIFIER

PROJECT PERFORMER + SOLE CUSTOMER ACCEPTANCE OWNER

MODEL OWNER + SOLE MODEL RISK APPROVER

AGENT DEVELOPER + SOLE PRODUCTION ACTIVATION APPROVER

DATA PRODUCER + SOLE DATA QUALITY VERIFIER

RISK MITIGATION OWNER + SOLE RESIDUAL RISK ACCEPTOR

RELEASE AUTHOR + SOLE RELEASE APPROVER + SOLE DEPLOYER
```

---

# 77. Conditional Responsibility Combinations

Some combinations may be allowed for low-risk work when:

- scope is limited;
- value is low;
- reversibility is high;
- monitoring is active;
- evidence is complete;
- a time-bounded exception exists;
- Human oversight is available.

Conditional combinations must not become default practice.
