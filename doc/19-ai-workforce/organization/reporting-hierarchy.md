# Mianx.ai Enterprise Reporting Hierarchy Standard

> **This document defines the governed target-state hierarchy through which
> Humans, AI Agents, Teams, Departments, Products, Projects, Customer
> Editions, Tenants, Governance bodies, executives, and the Founder may
> exchange operational, strategic, financial, Security, privacy, legal,
> compliance, Risk, performance, capacity, incident, and decision-support
> information without confusing reporting with authority, approval,
> accountability, delegation, or Production authorization.**

# 3. Exact Strategic Hierarchy

The Reporting Hierarchy must preserve:

```text
Mianx.ai Company and Governance
↓
MianX Core Platform
↓
Mianx.ai AI Operating System
↓
Shared AI Workforce
↓
Industry Operating Systems
↓
Customer Editions
↓
Autonomous Enterprise Creation at Scale
```

Reporting must not allow:

- a Customer Edition to report as the whole Company;
- an Industry Operating System to redefine enterprise strategy;
- a Project report to override Product ownership;
- a Department report to create Founder approval;
- an Agent report to represent itself as executive authority;
- one Customer or Tenant report to expose another Customer or Tenant;
- operational metrics to override Governance controls.

# 7. Reporting Definition

Reporting is the governed transmission of structured information from one
authorized source to one or more authorized recipients for:

- visibility;
- coordination;
- review;
- oversight;
- decision support;
- accountability tracking;
- Risk control;
- escalation;
- assurance;
- evidence.

A valid reporting relationship must define:

- reporter;
- recipient;
- subject;
- purpose;
- scope;
- cadence;
- format;
- Data sources;
- confidentiality;
- review;
- escalation;
- evidence.

---

# 8. Reporting Authority

Reporting authority defines who may:

- request a report;
- define a report;
- create a report;
- review a report;
- approve a report;
- distribute a report;
- change a reporting relationship;
- suspend a report;
- correct a report;
- archive a report;
- require escalation.

Reporting authority must be:

- explicit;
- current;
- scope-bounded;
- Product-specific;
- Project-specific;
- Customer-specific where applicable;
- Tenant-specific where applicable;
- environment-specific;
- time-bounded where delegated;
- evidence-backed;
- revocable.

---

# 9. Founder Reporting Authority

The Founder may receive reporting concerning:

- enterprise Vision;
- enterprise strategy;
- portfolio;
- executive performance;
- major Product direction;
- major Project status;
- enterprise finance;
- critical Security;
- critical privacy;
- legal and compliance;
- material Customer matters;
- existential Risk;
- Production authorization;
- major incidents.

Founder reporting must not be fabricated or represented as Founder approval.

---

# 10. Human Accountability Boundary

Qualified Human accountability remains mandatory for reporting involving:

- material financial statements;
- legal interpretation;
- employment matters;
- Customer contractual commitments;
- critical Security;
- critical privacy;
- regulated activity;
- Production release readiness;
- material residual Risk;
- Founder-reserved matters.

AI-generated reporting may support but not replace required Human review.

# 17. Reporting Hierarchy Levels

```text
RH-0 — Founder and Ownership Reporting

RH-1 — Enterprise Governance Reporting

RH-2 — Human Executive Reporting

RH-3 — Platform and Shared AI Workforce Reporting

RH-4 — Department Reporting

RH-5 — Product and Project Reporting

RH-6 — Customer Edition and Tenant Reporting

RH-7 — Team Reporting

RH-8 — Human and AI Agent Reporting

RH-9 — Workflow, Task, Metric, and Evidence Reporting
```

Lower levels must not bypass higher-level authority where escalation is
required.

---

# 18. RH-0 — Founder Reporting

Founder reporting should focus on:

- strategic exceptions;
- material outcomes;
- major portfolio changes;
- material financial position;
- critical Security and privacy;
- major legal and compliance matters;
- material Customer or Tenant impact;
- executive accountability;
- major unresolved Risks;
- decisions requiring Founder authority.

Founder reports should avoid unnecessary operational noise.

---

# 19. RH-1 — Governance Reporting

Governance reporting may include:

- policy compliance;
- control effectiveness;
- audit findings;
- Security posture;
- privacy posture;
- legal and compliance status;
- enterprise Risk;
- quality assurance;
- architecture assurance;
- Production readiness;
- exceptions;
- incidents.

Governance reports must preserve required independence.

---

# 20. RH-2 — Executive Reporting

Executive reports should support:

- enterprise coordination;
- strategy execution;
- portfolio decisions;
- Department performance;
- Product performance;
- Project performance;
- Customer health;
- financial control;
- capacity planning;
- major escalation;
- organizational change.

Executive reporting must distinguish information from decision authority.

---

# 21. RH-3 — Platform and Shared AI Workforce Reporting

This level may report on:

- Core Platform;
- AI Operating System;
- Agent registry;
- capability registry;
- Tool and Model usage;
- orchestration;
- workflow execution;
- memory;
- capacity;
- cost;
- quality;
- Security;
- evidence;
- Agent lifecycle;
- runtime incidents.

Planned Agents must not be reported as active Agents.

---

# 22. RH-4 — Department Reporting

Department reporting may include:

- mandate delivery;
- Department KPIs;
- Team performance;
- Human capacity;
- Agent capacity;
- Product support;
- Project support;
- Customer responsibilities;
- budget;
- dependencies;
- Risks;
- incidents;
- evidence gaps.

---

# 23. RH-5 — Product and Project Reporting

Product reporting should cover long-term Product value and health.

Project reporting should cover time-bounded delivery and closure.

The two must not be combined in a way that hides:

- Product ownership;
- Project scope;
- Customer commitments;
- budget;
- schedule;
- acceptance;
- unresolved Risk.

---

# 24. RH-6 — Customer Edition and Tenant Reporting

Customer and Tenant reporting must preserve:

- Customer identity;
- Customer Edition identity;
- Tenant identity;
- Product reference;
- Project reference;
- Data boundary;
- memory boundary;
- service scope;
- confidentiality;
- cost;
- incidents;
- acceptance.

Cross-Tenant aggregation requires approved anonymization or authorization.

---

# 25. RH-7 — Team Reporting

Team reporting may include:

- Team objectives;
- Task and workflow status;
- capacity;
- blockers;
- dependencies;
- quality;
- Product or Project contributions;
- incidents;
- review backlog;
- evidence.

Team activity must not be reported as accepted business value without
verification.

---

# 26. RH-8 — Human and AI Agent Reporting

Human and Agent reporting must preserve separate identities.

Human reporting may include:

- assigned outcomes;
- progress;
- Risks;
- capacity;
- decisions needed;
- evidence.

Agent reporting may include:

- Agent identity;
- Role;
- task status;
- Tool use;
- Model use;
- memory use;
- cost;
- confidence;
- limitations;
- errors;
- escalation;
- evidence.

---

# 27. RH-9 — Workflow, Task, Metric, and Evidence Reporting

This level includes:

- workflow state;
- Task state;
- queue state;
- completion state;
- review state;
- acceptance state;
- metric values;
- audit events;
- evidence references;
- incidents;
- exceptions.

Task completion must not be treated as accepted outcome without acceptance
evidence.

- Team to capability owner;
- Department to control function;
- Product Team to Product Department;

- review;
- recommend;
- require correction;
- block;
- escalate;
- approve.

- oversight;
- assurance;
- compliance;
- independent challenge;

- incident review;
