# Decision Making

> This document defines how decisions are proposed, evaluated, approved, communicated, and reviewed within the MIANX organization. It establishes a consistent framework that balances speed, accountability, transparency, and long-term business success.

Decision-making is not determined by hierarchy alone. Decisions should be made by the individual or team with the greatest ownership, expertise, and responsibility for the outcome.

---

# Purpose

The Decision Making framework ensures that organizational decisions are:

- Timely
- Transparent
- Data-driven
- Documented
- Accountable
- Aligned with company objectives

---

# Objectives

This document aims to:

- Define decision authority
- Reduce decision bottlenecks
- Empower teams
- Improve accountability
- Encourage collaboration
- Standardize approval workflows
- Maintain strategic alignment
- Document important decisions
- Support organizational scalability
- Enable continuous improvement

---

# Guiding Principles

Every decision should follow these principles:

- Customer First
- Ownership Before Authority
- Data Over Assumptions
- Transparency by Default
- Simplicity Over Complexity
- Long-Term Thinking
- Fast Feedback
- Continuous Learning

| Decision Type | Primary Owner |
|---------------|---------------|
| Company Vision | Founder |
| Company Strategy | Executive Leadership |
| Department Strategy | Department Head |
| Product Direction | Product Leadership |
| Technical Architecture | Engineering Leadership |

# Decision Process

```text
Identify Problem

↓

Gather Information

↓

Evaluate Options

↓

Assess Risks

↓

Make Decision

↓

Document Decision

↓

Communicate Decision

↓

Execute

↓

Review Outcome
```

Every significant decision should follow this lifecycle.

Decisions should consider:

- Business Goals

- Security

# Decision Documentation

Major decisions should include:

- Decision Summary
- Context
- Problem Statement
- Alternatives Considered
- Chosen Solution
- Risks
- Expected Outcomes
- Approval
- Review Date

# Delegation

Decision-making should be delegated whenever possible.

Managers should:

- Empower teams
- Avoid unnecessary approvals
- Encourage ownership
- Support informed decision-making

Delegation improves speed and develops future leaders.

---

# Escalation

Escalation is appropriate when:

- Risk is exceptionally high
- Cross-department agreement cannot be reached
- Budget limits are exceeded
- Legal or compliance concerns exist
- Strategic direction may change

Escalation should be rare and justified.

---

# Consensus vs Ownership

MIANX encourages collaboration but does not require unanimous agreement.

Decision model:

```text
Discuss Broadly

↓

Listen Carefully

↓

Decide Clearly

↓

Commit Fully
```

Once a decision is made, all stakeholders should support its execution.

---

# Decision Review

After implementation:

- Measure outcomes
- Compare against objectives
- Capture lessons learned
- Update documentation if necessary
- Improve future decisions

Learning from outcomes is part of the decision process.

---

# Common Decision Frameworks

Teams may use:

- Cost-Benefit Analysis
- Risk Assessment
- SWOT Analysis
- Technical RFCs
- Architecture Decision Records (ADR)
- Proof of Concept (PoC)

Framework selection depends on decision complexity.

---

# Best Practices

Recommended:

- Make decisions at the lowest responsible level
- Base decisions on evidence
- Document major decisions
- Encourage constructive disagreement
- Review outcomes regularly
- Avoid unnecessary approvals
- Communicate decisions clearly
- Learn from failures

---

# Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Decision paralysis
- Micromanagement
- Decisions without data
- Hidden decision-making
- Reversing decisions without review
- Ignoring stakeholder input
- Excessive approval chains
- Undefined ownership
