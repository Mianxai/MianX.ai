# Reporting Structure

> This document defines the official reporting structure of the MIANX organization. It establishes reporting relationships, management responsibilities, escalation paths, communication flow, and organizational accountability.

A clear reporting structure enables efficient decision-making, transparent communication, and scalable organizational growth.

---

# Purpose

The Reporting Structure defines how employees, teams, managers, and executives interact within the organizational hierarchy while ensuring accountability at every level.

---

# Objectives

The reporting structure aims to:

- Define reporting relationships
- Clarify management responsibilities
- Establish accountability
- Improve communication
- Reduce organizational confusion
- Support workforce scalability
- Enable efficient decision-making
- Maintain operational consistency
- Encourage leadership development
- Simplify organizational governance

---

# Guiding Principles

The reporting structure follows these principles:

- One Direct Manager
- Clear Accountability
- Transparent Communication
- Delegated Authority
- Ownership Over Hierarchy
- Leadership Through Service
- Cross-Team Collaboration
- Minimal Bureaucracy

---

# Reporting Hierarchy

```text
Founder / CEO

↓

Executive Leadership

↓

Department Heads

↓

Engineering Managers

↓

Team Leads

↓

Individual Contributors

↓

Interns & Trainees
```

Every employee belongs to a clearly defined reporting chain.

---

# Executive Reporting

Executive leaders report directly to the Founder / CEO.

Typical executive positions include:

- Chief Technology Officer (CTO)
- Chief Product Officer (CPO)
- Chief Operating Officer (COO)
- Chief Financial Officer (CFO)
- Chief Marketing Officer (CMO)

Responsibilities include:

- Company Strategy
- Department Oversight
- Executive Decisions
- Resource Allocation
- Organizational Growth

---

# Department Reporting

Department Heads report to their respective executives.

Each Department Head is responsible for:

- Department Strategy
- Team Management
- Budget Planning
- Performance
- Hiring
- Operational Excellence

Departments maintain autonomy while aligning with company objectives.

---

# Team Reporting

Engineering Managers report to Department Heads.

Team Leads report to Engineering Managers.

Engineers report to Team Leads or Engineering Managers depending on team size.

Example:

```text
Engineering Director

↓

Engineering Manager

↓

Backend Team Lead

↓

Senior Backend Engineer

↓

Backend Engineer

↓

Junior Engineer

↓

Intern
```

This structure ensures clear technical ownership.

---

# Individual Contributors

Individual Contributors (ICs):

- Report to one manager
- Own technical execution
- Collaborate across teams
- Participate in planning
- Mentor junior employees where appropriate

ICs are not required to become managers to advance their careers.

---

# Matrix Collaboration

Project collaboration may occur across departments.

Example:

```text
Backend Team
      │
      ├────────────┐
      │            │
Platform Team   AI Team
      │            │
      └────────────┘
```

Project collaboration does **not** change reporting relationships.

Employees continue reporting to their assigned manager.

---

# Communication Flow

Daily communication:

```text
Engineer

↓

Team Lead

↓

Engineering Manager

↓

Department Head
```

Strategic communication:

```text
Founder

↓

Executive Leadership

↓

Department Heads

↓

Managers

↓

Teams
```

Feedback flows in both directions.

---

# Escalation Path

Operational issues should follow this order:

```text
Employee

↓

Team Lead

↓

Engineering Manager

↓

Department Head

↓

Executive Leadership

↓

Founder (If Required)
```

Escalation should occur only when resolution cannot happen at the current level.

---

# Responsibilities by Level

| Level | Primary Responsibility |
|---------|------------------------|
| Founder | Vision & Company Direction |
| Executive | Business Strategy |
| Department Head | Department Success |
| Manager | Team Delivery |
| Team Lead | Technical Leadership |
| Individual Contributor | Execution & Ownership |

---

# Decision Authority

| Decision Type | Owner |
|---------------|-------|
| Company Vision | Founder |
| Company Strategy | Executive Leadership |
| Department Strategy | Department Heads |
| Team Planning | Managers |
| Technical Design | Team Leads |
| Implementation | Engineers |

Decision-making should occur at the lowest responsible level.

---

# Span of Control

Recommended team sizes:

| Role | Recommended Reports |
|------|---------------------|
| Team Lead | 5–8 |
| Engineering Manager | 8–15 |
| Department Head | 3–8 Managers |
| Executive | Multiple Departments |

These recommendations support effective leadership and communication.

---

# Reporting Rules

Every employee should:

- Have one direct manager
- Know their reporting line
- Understand performance expectations
- Participate in regular one-on-one meetings
- Receive continuous feedback

Reporting relationships should be documented and maintained.

---

# Performance Reporting

Managers are responsible for:

- Goal Tracking
- Performance Reviews
- Career Development
- Coaching
- Resource Planning
- Team Health

Performance discussions should occur regularly, not only during annual reviews.

---

# Organizational Changes

Changes to reporting structures should include:

- Business Justification
- Leadership Approval
- Employee Communication
- Documentation Updates
- Transition Planning

Frequent unnecessary restructuring should be avoided.

---

# Best Practices

Recommended:

- Maintain one direct reporting line
- Delegate authority appropriately
- Encourage open communication
- Conduct regular one-on-one meetings
- Document organizational changes
- Review reporting structures annually
- Empower technical leaders
- Resolve issues at the lowest possible level

---

# Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Multiple direct managers
- Undefined reporting relationships
- Micromanagement
- Unclear accountability
- Hidden organizational structures
- Excessive approval chains
- Frequent reporting changes
- Decision bottlenecks

---

# Future Evolution

As MIANX grows, the reporting structure may evolve to include:

- Regional Leadership
- Global Engineering Organizations
- Business Units
- Program Management Offices
- AI-Assisted Workforce Planning
- Skills-Based Team Assignments
- Dynamic Cross-Functional Squads

The reporting model should remain simple, transparent, and scalable.
