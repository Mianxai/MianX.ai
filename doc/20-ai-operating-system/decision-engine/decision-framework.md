# Mianx.ai AI Operating System Decision Framework

> **This document defines the governed Decision Framework through which the
> Mianx.ai AI Operating System converts evidence, Context, policies,
> constraints, options, uncertainty, risk, Human authority, Agent
> recommendations, and approved decision rights into traceable enterprise
> Decisions.**
>
> **A Decision is not the same as a recommendation, Approval, execution, or
> successful outcome. The AI OS must preserve these boundaries explicitly.**
>
> **Agents may recommend, evaluate, rank, or execute bounded Decisions only
> where authority has been explicitly granted. Founder sovereignty, Human
> accountability, Enterprise Governance, Security, legal obligations,
> Customer boundaries, and Production authorization remain superior to
> Agent reasoning or consensus.**
>
> **This document defines target-state governance and architecture. It does
> not prove that a Decision Engine runtime, autonomous Decision authority,
> Decision Registry, confidence calibration system, Production Decision
> enforcement, or Production authorization currently exists.**

# 10. Founder Sovereignty

The Founder retains ultimate authority over Founder-reserved Decisions
subject to applicable law and binding obligations.

No Agent, Agent group, Workflow, Model, or Decision Engine may supersede
Founder-reserved authority.

- Company mission;
- Company constitutional changes;
- fundamental ownership/governance changes;
- enterprise-wide autonomy changes;
- foundational AI OS authority changes;
- major strategic commitments;
- explicit Production authorization where designated;
- other reserved matters established by Governance.

The exact reserved registry requires formal approval.

# 21. Decision vs Approval

A Decision answers:

```text
WHAT SHOULD / WILL BE DONE?
```

Approval answers:

```text
IS THIS AUTHORIZED TO PROCEED?
```

Some Decisions may themselves be approvals, but the concepts must remain
explicitly modeled.

---

# 22. Decision vs Execution

A Decision defines an authorized direction.

Execution performs the resulting action.

---

# 97. Routine Bounded Decisions

# 98. Human-Required Decisions

A Decision is Human-required when policy or risk class demands direct Human

---

# 99. Human-in-the-Loop

Human-in-the-loop means the Human must intervene before the Decision or

---

# 100. Human-on-the-Loop

Human-on-the-loop means bounded autonomous Decisions may proceed while a
Human retains oversight and intervention authority.

---

# 101. Human-out-of-the-Loop

Human-out-of-the-loop operation may be appropriate only for explicitly
approved bounded cases.

It must not be assumed merely because an Agent can execute independently.

---

# 102. Human Oversight Boundary

```text
HUMAN CAN VIEW DASHBOARD
≠
MEANINGFUL HUMAN OVERSIGHT
```

Oversight must provide actionable intervention capability where required.

# 103. Agent Recommendation

- one recommendation;

# 104. Bounded Agent Decision

AGENT DECISION ELIGIBLE

# 105. Eligibility Boundary

Even when eligible:

```text
AGENT DECISION ELIGIBLE
≠
AGENT MUST DECIDE
```

# 193. Decision Anti-Gaming

Do not improve Decision metrics by:

- hiding escalations;
- suppressing Human overrides;
- reclassifying high-risk Decisions as routine;
- excluding failed autonomous Decisions;
- inflating confidence;
- removing rejected alternatives;
- hiding uncertainty;
- ignoring cross-Customer denial attempts;
- treating lucky outcomes as proof of good Decision quality.

---

# 194. Anti-Pattern — Agent Confidence as Authority

Prohibited:

```text
confidence = 0.99
THEREFORE
Agent may decide
```

without required authority.

---

# 195. Anti-Pattern — Consensus as Approval

Prohibited:

```text
5 Agents agree
THEREFORE
Approved
```

---

# 196. Anti-Pattern — Hidden Human Approval

Do not convert:

```text
Human viewed recommendation
```

into:

```text
Human approved recommendation
```

without explicit Approval.

---

# 197. Anti-Pattern — Decision in Free Text Only

Material Decisions should not exist only as unstructured chat text without
traceable identity and status.

---

# 198. Anti-Pattern — Select Then Rationalize

Decision systems must not:

```text
SELECT OPTION FIRST
↓
INVENT RATIONALE AFTERWARD
```

as a substitute for governed evaluation.

---

# 199. Anti-Pattern — Ignore No-Action Option

Forcing a choice between bad alternatives can create unnecessary risk.

---

# 200. Anti-Pattern — Permanent Temporary Decision

Temporary exceptions should not silently become permanent.

---

# 201. Anti-Pattern — Cross-Customer Decision Leakage

A Decision for Customer A must not be reused for Customer B merely because
business conditions appear similar.

---

# 202. Anti-Pattern — Decision Equals Execution

Do not allow a Decision Record alone to directly perform an irreversible
action without Execution controls.

---

# 203. Prohibited Decision Behaviors

The AI OS must not:

- allow Agents to self-grant Decision authority;
- treat recommendation as Decision;
- treat Decision as Approval automatically;
- treat Agent consensus as Human authority;
- hide uncertainty;
- fabricate confidence;
- bypass hard constraints;
- bypass Founder-reserved authority;
- bypass required Human review;
- bypass required Approval;
- allow Customer A Decision scope to affect Customer B without authority;
- allow Tenant A Decision scope to affect Tenant B without authority;
- accept expired Decision authority;
