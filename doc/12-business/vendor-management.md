# Vendor Management

---

# Purpose

The Enterprise Vendor Management Framework defines how MIANX-AI identifies, evaluates, selects, manages, monitors, and continuously improves relationships with external vendors, suppliers, contractors, technology providers, cloud providers, and service partners.

The framework ensures vendors deliver high-quality products and services while maintaining security, compliance, financial efficiency, and long-term strategic value.

---

# Objectives

The Vendor Management Framework aims to:

- Build reliable vendor relationships.
- Reduce operational risk.
- Improve procurement efficiency.
- Ensure regulatory compliance.
- Optimize vendor costs.
- Improve service quality.
- Strengthen business continuity.
- Standardize vendor governance.
- Monitor vendor performance.
- Enable long-term strategic partnerships.

---

# Scope

This framework applies to:

- Technology Vendors
- Cloud Providers
- Software Vendors
- AI Service Providers
- Infrastructure Providers
- Security Vendors
- Consulting Firms
- Outsourcing Partners
- Hardware Suppliers
- Professional Service Providers

# Vendor Risk Assessment

Every vendor shall undergo risk evaluation.

Assessment areas include:

- Cybersecurity Risk
- Financial Risk
- Operational Risk
- Compliance Risk
- Data Privacy Risk
- Geographic Risk
- Business Continuity
- Supply Chain Risk

Risk ratings:

- Low
- Medium
- High
- Critical

# Vendor Due Diligence

Due diligence includes:

- Company Verification
- Financial Review
- Security Assessment
- Compliance Verification
- Legal Review
- Reference Checks

# Best Practices

Vendor managers should:

- Build strategic partnerships.
- Monitor performance continuously.
- Review contracts regularly.
- Perform periodic risk assessments.
- Maintain vendor scorecards.
- Ensure regulatory compliance.
- Optimize vendor costs.
- Encourage innovation.

---

# Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Single vendor dependency
- Weak contracts
- Missing SLAs
- Poor security reviews
- Manual vendor tracking
- Delayed performance reviews
- Lack of risk monitoring
- Poor communication
- Uncontrolled vendor access
- Missing documentation

---

# Governance

The Enterprise Vendor Management Framework is governed by:

- Chief Procurement Officer (CPO)
- Chief Financial Officer (CFO)
- Executive Leadership Team
- Procurement Department
- Legal Department
- Security Department

The framework shall be reviewed quarterly and updated annually or whenever significant regulatory, operational, or business changes occur.
